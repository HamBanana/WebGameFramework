### **Acca Game Design Document**

#### **1. Game Overview**

**Acca** is a 3D board game with a top-down perspective focusing on investments, resource management, and strategic competition. Players navigate a grid, establishing properties, building businesses, and competing to control regions. The game is designed to be fully playable with a controller, offering a rich blend of economic strategy and territorial domination.

---

#### **2. Core Gameplay Mechanics**

**2.1 Grid Navigation**

- Players move across a grid of cells using a controller.
- Movement is restricted to adjacent cells, defined by neighbors (`up`, `down`, `left`, `right`).
- Special cells include:
  - **Chance Events**: Trigger random events impacting players.
  - **Markets**: Allow players to buy resources or items.

**2.2 Properties and Businesses**

- Most cells can host properties, which cost money to establish.
- Properties belong to a player’s company and can host multiple businesses.
- Businesses require resources to build and maintain, and employees to operate.

**2.3 Regional Control and Mayor Role**

- Owning all properties in a region grants the player the title of Mayor.
- Mayors collect taxes from the region’s population, tied to their happiness and size.
- Unhappy populations may migrate to other regions, reducing tax income.

**2.4 Population Management**

- Each region has a population count that determines available employees.
- Population happiness is influenced by:
  - Tax rates set by the Mayor.
  - Employment opportunities.
  - Regional amenities and random events.

**2.5 Trading and Interaction**

- Players can trade resources, properties, and businesses.
- Limited adversarial actions, like hostile takeovers, allow strategic disruption of opponents.

---

#### **3. Components and Their Roles**

**3.1 Cells**

- Basic units of the grid, hosting properties or special squares.
- Linked to neighbors for player navigation.

**3.2 Properties**

- Owned by players and tied to a company.
- Can host businesses that generate income and impact regional value.
- Properties can be sold to or bought from other players.

**3.3 Businesses**

- Built within properties, requiring resources and money.
- Provide jobs for the regional population and contribute to regional value.

**3.4 Regions**

- Groups of cells with unique traits and a population count.
- Regional value is influenced by properties, businesses, and population happiness.

**3.5 Companies**

- Players start with one company but can create more to specialize in different industries.
- Companies group properties and provide bonuses based on business types.

**3.6 Population**

- Determines employee availability for businesses.
- Size and happiness affect taxes and migration behavior.

---

#### **4. Planned Features**

**4.1 Mayor Role**

- Collect taxes from the population of the controlled region.
- Adjust tax rates, balancing income and happiness.
- Lose mayoral status if another player takes over properties or the population migrates away.

**4.2 Resource Management**

- Resource types (e.g., wood, steel, electricity) are required for building and maintaining businesses.
- Market prices fluctuate based on supply and demand.

**4.3 Chance Events**

- Introduce random, impactful scenarios:
  - "Stock Market Crash": Lose 10% of cash.
  - "Regional Festival": Boost happiness by 10%.

**4.4 Trading and Sabotage**

- Players can negotiate trades or perform hostile takeovers of properties at a multiplier of their value.
- Introduce sabotage mechanics, such as temporary property shutdowns.

**4.5 Businesses and Employees**

- Businesses scale from small shops to large factories, each requiring varying employee numbers.
- Idle businesses reduce happiness and regional value.

**4.6 Visual and Audio Feedback**

- Highlight active cells and properties.
- Add sound effects for movement, events, and interactions.
- Provide a UI overview of player performance and regional status.

**4.7 Multiplayer Modes**

- Competitive mode: Highest regional value wins.
- Cooperative mode: Players collaborate to achieve shared objectives.

#### **5. Implementation Roadmap**

**Phase 1: Core Systems**

- Implement grid navigation and cell linking.
- Establish properties and link them to regions.
- Add player movement and controller integration.

**Phase 2: Population and Businesses**

- Develop population dynamics, happiness, and migration.
- Introduce businesses with resource and employee requirements.

**Phase 3: Regional Control and Mayor Role**

- Implement regional ownership mechanics and tax systems.
- Create a happiness management system with player-adjustable tax rates.

**Phase 4: Interaction and Events**

- Add chance events and trading systems.
- Implement sabotage mechanics and dynamic property purchases.

**Phase 5: Visual and Audio Feedback**

- Add visual highlights, animations, and sound effects.
- Create menus for tax adjustments, business upgrades, and resource management.

**Phase 6: Multiplayer and Scenarios**

- Introduce competitive and cooperative multiplayer modes.
- Add predefined game scenarios with unique objectives.

#### **6. Open Questions**

- How can we balance the tax system to avoid punishing players for high population growth?
- Should migration between regions allow opposing players to capitalize on incoming populations?
- How will properties and businesses handle endgame scenarios (e.g., auctions for final properties)?
